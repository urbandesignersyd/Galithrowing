Wahroonga Written & Wonderful Word Works

English has gaps where some ideas should be. This lexicon gathers the words the practice has coined to fill them, together with the German, Japanese and French terms we borrow when another language has already found the right word.

Coined

The first coined entries are being prepared and will be published here.

Borrowed

Ma間/mah/Japanese
The charged interval between things: a pause, gap or negative space that gives form its meaning.“The courtyard is not empty; it is the building’s ma.”
Shakkei借景/shahk-kay/Japanese
Borrowed scenery; incorporating distant landscape into a composed view.“The pavilion’s western window practises shakkei with the ridge line.”
Wabi-sabi侘寂/wah-bee sah-bee/Japanese
Beauty found in impermanence, imperfection and the patina of age.“Specify materials that will gather wabi-sabi rather than merely deteriorate.”
Komorebi木漏れ日/koh-moh-reh-bee/Japanese
Sunlight filtering through the leaves of trees.“The canopy plan is, above all, a plan for komorebi.”
Mono no aware物の哀れ/moh-noh noh ah-wah-reh/Japanese
A gentle, wistful awareness of the transience of things.
Gesamtkunstwerk/geh-ZAHMT-koonst-vairk/German
A total work of art that unites architecture, objects, landscape and experience.
Fingerspitzengefühl/FING-er-shpitz-en-geh-fyool/German
Intuitive, fingertip sensitivity; the tacit judgement of an experienced maker.“No specification replaces the joiner’s Fingerspitzengefühl.”
Waldeinsamkeit/VAHLT-ine-zahm-kite/German
The solitary, reflective feeling of being alone in the woods.
Dépaysement/day-pay-eez-mahn/French
The disorientation, often welcome, of being somewhere unfamiliar.“A threshold designed to induce a moment of dépaysement.”
Flânerie/flah-nuh-ree/French
Unhurried, observant wandering through the city.“Streets should reward flânerie at walking pace.”
Bricolage/bree-koh-lahzh/French
Construction from whatever materials are to hand; resourceful making.
